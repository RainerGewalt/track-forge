# Code of Conduct

Track Forge is a small project used by people from the live-coding and music
community, many of whom are new to code, to Strudel or to open source. This
code of conduct applies to issues, pull requests, discussions and every other
project space, and to anyone representing the project elsewhere.

## Expected behavior

- Be patient and kind, especially with people who are just starting out.
- Discuss code and music, not the person. Critique is welcome when it is
  specific and respectful.
- Respect different musical choices. There is no right genre or style here.
- Accept a "no" to a feature or change gracefully. The scope of the project is
  deliberately small.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation or discrimination of any kind, including on the
  basis of age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression,
  level of experience, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or
  sexual identity and orientation.
- Sexualized language or imagery, insults, and personal or political attacks.
- Publishing other people's private information without their explicit
  permission.
- Repeatedly disrupting discussions.

## Reporting

All project spaces are public pages on GitHub, so the maintainer sees problems
there and acts on them under the enforcement rules below.

To report something confidentially, such as harassment or threats, use
GitHub's own reporting: **Report content** in the options menu of an issue,
pull request or comment, or **Block or Report** on a user's profile. These
reports go to GitHub's staff, who assess them against GitHub's rules, not
against this code of conduct; see
[reporting abuse or spam](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).
The project does not have a private contact address for conduct reports yet.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may edit, hide or reject comments, commits, issues and pull
requests that violate this code of conduct, and may block a person from the
project temporarily or permanently. The response depends on the severity and
can range from a private note to a public warning or a ban.
